The status quo

Most voice agents stop improving the day they ship.

Building the agent is the easy part. What breaks teams is everything after launch — and it happens in tools that don't talk to each other.

Failures hide in the long tail

A handful of calls go wrong the same way every week. Nobody sees the pattern because nobody can listen to ten thousand calls.

QA is a sampling problem

Teams review a few dozen conversations a month and hope they were representative. They rarely are.

Fixes are guesses

Someone edits a prompt, ships it, and waits. There is no test that proves the change helped, or that it broke something else.

The tools are disconnected

Building, monitoring, evaluating and improving live in four products. The loop never closes.

Capabilities

Built for the two hours after something goes wrong.

Automated evaluation on every call

Every conversation is scored against your own criteria — resolution, compliance, tone, verification, escalation correctness — the moment it ends. No sampling, no monthly QA batch.

Simulation and regression testing

Replay a proposed change against real historical calls before it goes live, and keep a regression set that has to pass on every future change.

Natural turn-taking

Interruption, barge-in and backchannel handled as conversation, not as errors.

Real backend actions

Tool calls into booking, payments, CRM and ticketing — with results read back accurately.

Guardrails and escalation

Hard boundaries the agent cannot cross, and explicit rules for when a human takes over.

Multilingual

One agent definition, many languages, with per-language quality tracked separately.
